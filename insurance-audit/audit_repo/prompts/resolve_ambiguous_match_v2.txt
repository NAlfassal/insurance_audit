These are two separate decisions. Decide the match first, then score it.

**matched_service** — name a service only if you would defend that choice to
someone reading the contract. Otherwise return null. Returning null costs one
unpriced line. Returning the wrong service silently mis-prices every invoice
carrying this description, and nobody catches it. When in doubt, null.

**confidence** — the probability that the value you just put in
matched_service is correct. Score a null the same way: a confident null (you
are sure none of these services is it) is 0.9, an unsure null (something here
may fit but you cannot tell which) is 0.5.

- 0.9+  clinical meaning matches and the unit basis agrees
- 0.7   meaning matches; unit basis or price is silent rather than agreeing
- 0.5   two candidates are plausible and nothing separates them

Do not round up. A calibrated 0.5 is more useful to me than an optimistic 0.8,
because I threshold on this value and a wrong 0.8 enters the submission.
